#1.5#Subscriptions, Helpdesk & Marketing#/#
Hello everyone. Today, let’s look at three Odoo modules: **Subscriptions**, **Helpdesk**, and **Marketing**.
So, let’s start with ᴃᴃSubscriptionsᴃᴃ
​
#1.5#Subscriptions#/#
​
##1. Subscription dashboard##
A subscription can also provide ongoing access to a digital service. For this example, we’ll sell **Cloud Storage**: a monthly plan that gives a customer a set amount of secure online storage. Let’s set up the service, sell it, and follow the subscription.
​
ᴃᴃSubscriptions ➔ Dashboardᴃᴃ
So here we are on the Subscriptions dashboard. Let's start with the records and statuses shown here. This gives us a quick view of the subscriptions the company is managing.
​
Let's open a subscription. Here, we can see the customer, product, recurring plan, and order information. These details tell us what the customer bought and what happens next.
​
You can see that the subscription is connected to a sales order. So we can follow the customer’s recurring service through familiar Odoo sales information.
​
##2. Configure the recurring plan and product##
Before we create the Cloud Storage product, let's set up the plans that define how often customers are billed and what they can manage themselves.

ᴃᴃSubscriptions ➔ Configuration ➔ Recurring Plans ➔ Newᴃᴃ
Let's create a plan called **Monthly**. The form has a few settings that control its billing schedule and the choices available to customers in the portal.

**Billing Period** is set to **1 Month**. This means the subscription is billed once each month. If we wanted quarterly billing, we could set the period to three months.

**Align to Period Start** is unchecked here. That means the customer’s billing cycle follows the subscription start date instead of aligning everyone to the first day of the calendar month. If we enable it, subscriptions are aligned to the start of the next billing period, and the first invoice can be adjusted for the partial period.

**Automatic Closing** is set to **15 Days**. If a recurring payment remains unpaid, Odoo waits this grace period and then closes the subscription automatically. This gives the customer time to resolve a payment issue before the plan is closed.

**Company** says **Visible to all**. In a database with multiple companies, that means this recurring plan can be used across all of them. We could select a specific company if the plan should only be available to one company.

**Invoice Email Template** is set to **Invoice: Sending**. This is the email template Odoo uses for subscription invoice communications, including details such as the customer and invoice information.

Now let's look at the **Self-Service** options. These decide which subscription changes customers can make themselves from the portal.

**Closable** is enabled and set to **At date**. This lets a customer close their subscription through the portal and choose when the closure takes effect.

**Pausable** is enabled, so a customer can pause the subscription for a while instead of closing it completely.

**Add Products** is unchecked. If we enabled it, customers could add products or change quantities on their recurring order, which can generate an upsell quotation.

**Renew** is enabled, so customers can create a renewal quotation from the portal when they want to continue.

Under **Optional Plans**, we see **Yearly**. This gives customers on the monthly plan the option to switch to the yearly plan. Odoo prepares a new or renewal quotation for that change.

For our demonstration, we’ll keep these settings as shown. The main idea is that the recurring plan controls the billing interval and the self-service choices available to the customer.

ᴃᴃSubscriptions ➔ Products ➔ Products ➔ Newᴃᴃ
Now let's create our product, **Cloud Storage**. It gives the customer ongoing access to secure online storage, billed monthly.

On the product form, enter the name and set **Product Type** to **Service**. When we create the product from the Subscriptions app, the subscription option is usually enabled by default; let's make sure it stays enabled. We can use the product description to explain the plan, for example, 500 GB of storage for a monthly fee.

Open the **Recurring Prices** tab and add a price rule for the **Monthly** plan. Enter the recurring price for the service. If we want customers to switch to **Yearly**, we can also add a yearly price rule here. Then save the product.

We don't need to go through every general product field. Let's focus on the service name, the storage amount included, and the recurring prices that match the plans we just configured.

##3. Create and confirm the customer quotation##
ᴃᴃSubscriptions ➔ Dashboard ➔ Newᴃᴃ
Now, let's create a quotation for Bailey Builds. On the quotation, select the customer and add **__Cloud Storage__** on the order line. Then choose the **__monthly recurring__** plan and **__review the quantity and recurring price__**.
​
Let's open **Other Info** and check the subscription start date. Then review the order line and confirm the recurring price and billing interval. The quotation should clearly show what storage service the customer is subscribing to and the monthly charge.
​
At this point, this is still a quotation. We can review it with the customer and make changes before confirming it. Once the customer accepts, let's click **Confirm**.
​
Now we can see that the quotation has become a confirmed sales order and the subscription is active. **MRR** means Monthly Recurring Revenue; it shows the monthly recurring revenue from this subscription.
​
##4. Renew or change a subscription##
Now, let’s look at what happens when a customer wants to continue or change their Cloud Storage plan.
​
Let's open an invoiced subscription. In this example, the order has already been invoiced, which is needed for the renewal workflow shown in the training material. Let's click **Renew** and review the new quotation Odoo creates for the same customer and product.
​
Before confirming the renewal, let's check the recurring plan and order lines again. Then let's confirm the new quotation and look at how the next period is recorded.
​
If the customer needs more space, we can use an ᴃᴃupsellᴃᴃ quotation to move them from a 500 GB plan to a 2 TB plan. That is an upsell because it expands the same service. A separate team collaboration tool could be a complementary cross-sell.
​
If the customer no longer wants Cloud Storage, we close the subscription and check that the status reflects that. The important thing on this screen is the current state of the subscription and the action we take next: renew, change, or close.
​
ᴃᴃChange Plan from Monthly to Yearlyᴃᴃ
Because **Yearly** appears under **Optional Plans**, let's open the customer preview and show the plan-switch option. When the customer chooses Yearly, Odoo prepares a quotation for the change. We can review the new billing period and price before confirming it.
​
##5. Subscription transition##
That was the subscription workflow: we set up a service product and monthly price, created a customer quotation, confirmed it, and followed it into invoicing. We also saw where to renew the subscription or switch from the Monthly plan to the Yearly plan.
​
Now let’s move to Helpdesk. A customer may need support if they cannot access their files or upload new ones. Let's see how Odoo turns that request into a ticket the team can track.
​
#1.5#Helpdesk#/#
​
##1. Helpdesk dashboard and teams##
Helpdesk is where the team organizes customer requests as tickets. A ticket keeps the issue, customer, owner, and progress together in one record.
​
ᴃᴃHelpdesk ➔ Dashboardᴃᴃ
Here we can see the Helpdesk teams. Let's open the team that handles customer support.
​
On the team page, let's click **Tickets** to open its pipeline. Then let's open the team settings and look at the team members. These settings tell us who can work on tickets for this team.
​
The exact teams depend on the company. For our example, a Cloud Support team could handle account access and syncing problems, while a General Support team could handle questions about plans and billing.
​
##2. Create and assign a ticket##
ᴃᴃHelpdesk ➔ Team ➔ Tickets ➔ Newᴃᴃ
Let's create a ticket for a customer who cannot upload files to Cloud Storage. Enter a short title, select the customer, and describe the issue—for example, the customer receives an error when uploading a file.
​
Notice the team and assigned person fields. They show who is responsible for the ticket. Let's select the Cloud Support team and assign an employee, then save the ticket.
​
Now we can see the saved ticket card in the pipeline. The team can see the issue and who is responsible for following up. If the customer contacts us through a configured email alias or website contact form, Odoo can also create a ticket from that channel. Let's look at the channel configuration only if it is already available in the demo database.
​
##3. Move the ticket through stages##
On this Kanban screen, each column is a stage and each card is a ticket. The stages describe the team’s process.
​
Let's move our ticket from **New** to **In Progress** to show that someone has started working on it. If we need more information from the customer, let's move it to a waiting stage. When the issue is resolved, let's move it to the solved or closed stage used by this team.
​
As the card moves, notice that the stage gives us a quick visual indicator. It helps the team see what needs attention without opening every ticket.
​
##4. Show the SLA on the team and ticket##
An SLA, or Service Level Agreement, is the target time for handling a ticket. It helps the company set a service expectation and helps the team see which requests need attention.
​
ᴃᴃHelpdesk ➔ Team Settingsᴃᴃ
Let's open the team settings and point out the SLA option and the working-hours setting. The working hours matter because the target should reflect when the team is expected to work.
​
ᴃᴃHelpdesk ➔ Configuration ➔ SLA Policiesᴃᴃ
Let's open a simple SLA policy. Notice the team, the conditions that determine which tickets qualify, the target stage, and the time limit. The exact labels may vary slightly in our database, so we'll focus on the fields visible on screen.
​
Now, let's return to a matching ticket and show the SLA information on the ticket. The ticket stage tells us where the work is; the SLA tells us whether we are meeting the time target. Those are related, but they answer different questions.
​
##5. Resolve a ticket and review customer feedback##
After a ticket is resolved, customer ratings can show how the support experience felt to the customer.
​
ᴃᴃHelpdesk ➔ Team Settings ➔ Customer Ratingsᴃᴃ
Let's open the team settings and find Customer Ratings. If there are responses, we can open the rating view and look at a rating and comment. This gives us direct feedback to review after closing the ticket.
​
Now let's finish by resolving the Cloud Storage access ticket and reviewing the customer feedback workflow.
​
ᴃᴃHelpdesk ➔ Open the Cloud Storage access ticketᴃᴃ
On the ticket, let's review the customer’s message and any troubleshooting notes. We can add a response, assign the next action, and move the ticket to **Solved** once the customer can upload files again.
​
After resolving the ticket, let's check whether a customer rating or comment is available. That lets the team see how the support experience went and whether the issue was fully resolved.
​
##6. Helpdesk transition##
We’ve followed one support ticket from creation to assignment, moved it through the pipeline, checked its SLA, and reviewed customer feedback.
​
Now, let's move to Marketing. We have a monthly Cloud Storage plan and a support process; Marketing helps us introduce the service and explain its benefits to the right audience.
​
#1.5#Marketing#/#
​
##1. Email Marketing dashboard##
Marketing helps us communicate with customers and potential customers. We'll focus on email mailings, campaigns, A/B testing, and a short automation example.
​
ᴃᴃEmail Marketing ➔ Dashboardᴃᴃ
Here we are on the email marketing dashboard. We can see the mailings and basic information in the list, such as the subject and status.
​
A **mailing** is one standalone email. A **campaign** groups related mailings into a broader sequence. For example, the first email can introduce Cloud Storage, and a later email can explain plan options or remind interested customers to sign up. Each email is a mailing; together they can be part of one campaign.
​
##2. Choose the audience##
ᴃᴃEmail Marketing ➔ Mailing Lists ➔ Mailing Listsᴃᴃ
Let's open the mailing lists and select a list of small businesses or customers interested in secure file storage. This is a relevant audience for Cloud Storage.
​
We want to send a useful message to the right people. Also, remember that if a contact has opted out, we must respect that preference.
​
##3. Prepare an email mailing##
ᴃᴃEmail Marketing ➔ Mailings ➔ Newᴃᴃ
Now, let’s create a mailing called “Cloud Storage.” First, let's enter the subject. Then, let's select the mailing list as the recipients and open the email editor.
​
On the editor screen, we can choose a building block or template. Let's add a heading, a short description of the plan, and one clear button or call to action. We’ll keep the message focused so customers quickly understand the offer.
​
Before sending, let's preview the email and check its layout, subject, recipients, and links. If available, let's send a test email to myself. We'll stop before sending to the full list unless this is an approved live campaign.
​
For the campaign walkthrough, let's open the campaign feature and look at where related mailings are grouped. If the feature is not enabled, let's check its setting first. Then we can compare the campaign with an individual mailing on screen.
​
##4. Compare versions with A/B testing##
If we are not sure which subject line or call to action works better, we can test two versions of the mailing.
​
ᴃᴃEmail Marketing ➔ Open a mailing ➔ A/B Testingᴃᴃ
Let's open the test setup and show the two versions. For example, one version can focus on secure backups, and the other on accessing files from anywhere. Then notice the test audience portion and the measure we want to compare, such as opens or clicks.
​
Odoo sends the versions to a portion of the recipients, compares the selected result, and can send the stronger version to the remaining audience. The key point is that the result depends on the measure we chose. More opens may not automatically mean more sales.
​
##5. Show a marketing automation campaign##
Marketing automation can trigger activities based on a defined audience and conditions. Let's use an existing test campaign so we can focus on the workflow rather than building every setting from scratch.
​
ᴃᴃMarketing Automation ➔ Campaigns ➔ Open a test campaignᴃᴃ
On the campaign screen, let's start with the target audience or filter. Then, let's open one activity and show what it does, such as sending an email or creating a follow-up task.
​
Let's look at the condition that controls when the next activity happens. Then, let's click the test option and choose a sample record. Let's review the result to check that the right record entered the workflow and that the expected activity was created.
​
The key point is that testing lets us catch mistakes before launching a campaign to real customers. After the test, we still need to review the message, audience, timing, and customer preferences before launch.
​
##6. Closing##
Let’s connect what we saw on screen.
​
In Subscriptions, we set up Cloud Storage as a recurring service and followed it through quotation, confirmation, invoicing, and renewal. In Helpdesk, we created an access-support ticket, assigned it to a team member, moved it through stages, checked its SLA, and reviewed customer feedback. In Marketing, we selected an audience, prepared an email, compared versions, and tested an automation workflow.
​
These modules support different steps in the customer relationship, and Odoo lets us connect the related information and processes.
